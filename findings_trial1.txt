CLINICAL RESEARCH FINDINGS REPORT
==================================
Study Title : Glycaemic Control in Type 2 Diabetes Cohort
Lead Researcher : bob_researcher
Date : 2025-04-18
Dataset Used : patient_dataset_trial1 (controlled / pseudonymised)

SUMMARY
-------
This report presents findings from analysis of the pseudonymised patient
dataset provided by the clinical team. All analysis was performed on
de-identified data in compliance with GDPR Article 9 requirements.

FINDINGS
--------
1. HbA1c Improvement
   - Mean HbA1c across the cohort: 58 mmol/mol (baseline)
   - After 12-week Metformin titration, mean HbA1c reduced by 12%
   - ANON_0001 showed the most significant response: 58 → 49 mmol/mol

2. Blood Pressure Management
   - ANON_0002 presented with hypertension (145/92 mmHg)
   - Amlodipine 5mg initiated; follow-up BP reading: 132/84 mmHg
   - Reduction of 13 mmHg systolic observed over 8 weeks

3. Renal Function Monitoring
   - ANON_0003 (CKD Stage 3): eGFR stable at 42 ml/min over study period
   - No further decline observed; Ramipril dose maintained
   - Recommend quarterly monitoring per NICE CKD guidelines

4. Respiratory Outcomes
   - ANON_0004 (Asthma): peak flow improved from 380 L/min to 410 L/min
   - No acute exacerbations reported during the study period

5. Cardiac Monitoring
   - ANON_0005 (Atrial Fibrillation): heart rate controlled at 88 bpm
   - INR within therapeutic range throughout; no adverse events recorded

STATISTICAL SUMMARY
-------------------
Cohort size        : 5 patients (pseudonymised)
Study duration     : 12 weeks
Primary endpoint   : Glycaemic control (HbA1c reduction)
Secondary endpoints: BP reduction, eGFR stability, peak flow improvement

Overall cohort response rate : 4/5 patients showed measurable improvement
Adverse events               : 0

CONCLUSION
----------
The controlled dataset analysis supports the hypothesis that combined
pharmacological management of comorbidities in a T2D cohort produces
measurable improvements across multiple biomarkers within 12 weeks.
Further investigation with a larger cohort (N >= 50) is recommended
before clinical recommendations are formalised.

COMPLIANCE NOTE
---------------
All data used in this analysis was pseudonymised prior to researcher
access. No direct patient identifiers were available to the research
team at any point. This report references only anonymised tokens
(ANON_XXXX) and aggregate statistics.

Data handling complies with:
  - GDPR Article 9 (special category health data)
  - GDPR Article 25 (data protection by design)
  - ICO Research Exemption guidance

END OF REPORT
